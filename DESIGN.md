# Design

The site is a ship's ledger beside a frozen sea. One storm, run three times by eight members, is twenty-four storms; the page writes each one's fate and confirms the edit only when enough pairs agree.

## What it takes, and from where

Lucas Pope's _Return of the Obra Dinn_ (2018): two inks and an ordered dither for every grey, a pocket watch that moves you between instants, a ledger of fates, and a confirmation that is withheld until enough of them agree. The paired signed-rank test behaves the same way: it refuses to run with fewer than five pairs, and its smallest p with n pairs is 1/2^n, reached only when every pair points the same way.

## Ground and ink

| Token      | Value     | Use                                             |
| ---------- | --------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| `--ground` | `#1d2290` | Page, relief shadow, the dark ink.              |
| `--ink`    | `#ece6d6` | Text, rules, the light ink. 10:1 on the ground. |

There are no other colours. Every grey is a dither in the relief, a dotted rule in the page, or a dotted line in a chart. The legacy tokens (`--fork`, `--control`, `--line` and the rest) all resolve to one of the two, so runs are told apart by line style: the fork solid, the control dotted, the baseline the rule at zero.

## Type

IM Fell English for every word: headings, prose, navigation, controls, captions and the names on the relief, run names and column heads in italic. Departure Mono only where a clerk would write figures: the ledger's entries (13 px; 11 px, the face's own size, on a narrow ledger), the watch's time, the stamp, and the counts on the edge arrows. IM Fell's figures are old-style, and its zero reads as a letter o, so prose takes its digits from EB Garamond (a 2 KB subset holding only 0 to 9, roman and italic): lining figures in a face of the same lineage. The test's figures under the stamp are prose in italic IM Fell, since the pixel face's comma reads as a full stop at that size; "p" and its value are joined by a non-breaking space. The stamp and the two run names on the relief are the only capitals: the stamp in mono, the names in roman IM Fell with wide spacing, since the italic U reads as a V. All three are OFL and bundled in `site/src/style/fonts` with their licences. The navigation is set as the ledger's running head: italic page names between middots over a double rule, the current page in roman. The closing links sit inside a sentence, not as a pair of buttons.

## The signature: the relief and the burn

`site/src/relief.ts` draws one run's real sea-level pressure (member 0's `mslp.png` atlas, written by `stormfork export`) as a landscape seen from the south at a 42° pitch (50° on a phone): lows sink as deep pits lit from the north-west, highs swell, land stands a step above the sea, and isobars ring every storm below 1000 hPa at 4 hPa. Every fifth ring (980, 960) is heavier and carries its value on a knocked-out label south of the storm; the rings between are thinner, so the low reads as terrain, not as a target. The relief fills its panel to every edge, aimed at 100°W to 32°W, with the watch and its controls on a plate knocked out of it. Sea and land are told apart by grain as well as tone: both are dotted through interleaved gradient noise, which spreads dots like blue noise and never forms a grid, the sea one pixel to a dot and the land two by two. Beyond 15 degrees of a live storm, land's step above the sea is halved along with the light. Within about 10 degrees of a live storm the light has full contrast; beyond 15 it is softened to half, so land, sea and the north-west light keep their steps while a distant low (the Iceland low in this run, or the rings left where a struck storm died) never outshouts Sandy. Rings go dotted at the edge of a storm's reach. The coast is a solid 1-pixel line in every frame, so a burn always has a coast to cross, the coast is drawn over any char, and no stretch of sea falls below three dots in a hundred, so none reads as a hole in the image. Five WebGL2 passes run at the canvas's own resolution, one relief pixel per 2 CSS px on every screen, the canvas a whole number of relief pixels wide so the dither never changes pitch:

1. the displaced surface, shaded from the smoothed field's own normals under a low north-west light;
2. the heads: a solid square in a knock-out at each live storm, crisp over its own fire, one per member, so five heads crossing a coast together stay five; no track is drawn as a line;
3. the heat field, ping-ponged: it cools within a tenth of a second and climbs in flickering 3-pixel columns, and each frame every storm's fire is stamped afresh, square stamps kept with a max blend. The falloff is set by distance along the track, in steps behind the storm (`heatAt` in `relief.ts`, tested in `relief.test.ts`): a short solid head (a fifth of a step), the inverted band (to one step), embers in 2-pixel blocks whose density falls with age (to six steps), then char: a knock-out to the ground, darker than any ground around it, rimmed by sparse embers, never cream. The ribbon narrows from 10 px to a 6 px scar as it cools. The coast is drawn over all of it. The ribbon replaces the track's line in play and in a still frame alike (reduced motion, the fallback image, the card), where all twenty-four ribbons are stamped once from the edit point, so no track is ever drawn as wire. A struck storm keeps a cold scar along its last stretch, and on the last step the control's scars join them, so the closing frame holds all twenty-four fates: a scar and the ledger's cross where each lost storm was last seen, and, for storms that left the map, an arrow inset 24 px from the frame's edge with its run and count in mono ("4 moved"), one arrow per run and direction. A storm's head is held at its last fix until the ledger strikes its row, and on that frame the whole fire chars;
4. the composite: hot pixels go solid light, cooler ones invert the relief under them (lifted to a half tone first, so the band shows on dark sea), cooler still they char to the dark ink with sparse 2-pixel embers, and the relief shimmers above a burn;
5. a blit that dissolves from the previous instant through the dither's own matrix, threshold by threshold, so the two images mix as tones with no moiré; while it runs, both runs' isobar rings and coasts stay whole.

The forecast plays at 2.6 steps a second, the frames between six-hour steps interpolated. At the first unedited landfall (30 Oct, 00:00 UTC) the play slows to 1.5 steps a second until the last unedited storm is lost. Five unedited heads cross the coast together on the unedited relief, their rows flashing one by one; a step later (30 Oct, 06:00) the relief dissolves over 1.1 s to the run moved 300 km east. The unedited fires burn inland and each chars on the frame its row is struck, while the eight moved fires keep burning out to sea. Hovering or focusing a ledger row burns that member's track alone, in 2 s, half as large again: the head, the inverted band and the char all readable in any frame, and what has burnt left charred with embers around a solid line for as long as the row is held. Meanwhile the rest of the relief sinks to half its tone and its lines thin to dots, so the fire is the only bright thing, and that lone ribbon's char keeps a brighter ember rim so its scar still reads on the dimmed sea.

Reduced motion renders one still at the last confirmed step (31 Oct, 06:00 UTC) with the moved run, no heat and no dissolve; every control still works and redraws one frame. Without WebGL the page shows `site/public/relief-still.png`, that same frame captured with its leaders, and the ledger and stamp open at that same step instead of playing.

## The ledger

One row per storm, grouped by run. Three fate words, the same at every step: "At sea", "Ashore" with the first fix over Natural Earth land, "Lost at sea" when the tracker loses it offshore. Each run's head counts them, and the three always add up to its eight members, as "at sea 3 · ashore 5 · lost 0": three counts that cannot read as contradicting each other. A storm the tracker no longer follows stays in full ink: a cross beside its member number, its fate word struck, and a dotted rule under the row. Columns have fixed widths and the pressures are set flush right, so no figure moves between instants; where the ledger is narrow, the whole table steps down to 11 px, one size in every cell, and a fate whose date will not fit takes a second line. When the fate of a storm on the relief is sealed during play its fate cell flashes in the light ink; cells sealed together queue, so one is lit at a time. The row under the pointer or the keyboard gets only a 2 px bar in its margin. The stamp block holds one height with the row of cells set directly above the stamp, so neither moves between states; it grows only on a ledger too narrow to set the figures in that room. The two run names on the relief sit at the ends of knocked-out leaders at different angles (the unedited name climbs steeply toward the land, the moved one runs flat out to sea), anchored at a live head, the run's member nearest its mean, with the name beyond the shown storm's outermost ring, placed clear of both storms, the ring values, the watch's plate and each other. The stamp reads "n of n confirmed" when p equals 1/2^n, "Confirmed" or "Not confirmed" otherwise, and "Unconfirmed" with a dashed border when the test does not run. The paired test holds from the first step, because the 300 km edit alone puts every moved storm farther from its twin than the control. Until the fates split (the first holding step with more unedited storms ashore than moved ones, 30 Oct 00:00 in this run) the stamp is a flat dotted outline reading "n of n apart", and the sentence beside it says this is still the edit, not a split. At the split it slams down in ink as "n of n confirmed" and names the split. Once fewer than five pairs remain the test is closed: the stamp keeps that verdict, adds "Test closed at n pairs", and its border turns double; the key over it reads "test closed at n pairs" instead of the live count. Every count of fates in the sentence comes from the same tallies as the ledger's heads at the step on show (`fatesLine` in `fates.ts`, tested against the published run), so after the close it reads "By 5 Nov 00:00: 8 unedited ashore, 1 moved" beside a ledger that says the same. The figures beside it give how far the moved storms grew apart beyond the 300 km edit, never the raw distance. The row of cells over it is the paired test at every step, captioned with the rule that the test runs while five or more pairs remain. The watch's face spreads eleven days, so the forecast's last day never points at its first. The social card carries the whole ledger beside the relief, with the wordmark and the repository address on a plate in the relief's corner. On a phone the page bar scrolls away with the page, so it never covers a ledger row, and it keeps the wordmark. The lede's cross explains the ledger's: it marks a storm the tracker no longer follows. The watch's plate is edged with a hairline.

## Rules

1. One number per quantity, formatted once (`formatKm`, `formatP`, `stepTime` in `site/src/steps.ts`).
2. The canvas never holds the only copy of a fact: the ledger, the stamp, the tags and the step table carry them as text.
3. No raw field names or JSON on screen. A missing run gets one sentence and the command that fetches it.
4. Contrast at WCAG AA, a visible focus ring on every control, and a keyboard path through all of them: the watch is a slider (arrows, Page Up and Down, Home, End), each ledger row's member number is a button.
5. Steady 60 fps at 1440 by 900; the loop pauses when the relief is off screen or the tab is hidden.
6. Small screens: the relief sits above the ledger; the atlas controls move to a bottom sheet.

## What must not change

Two inks. The ledger's order and wording of fates. The stamp's refusal below five pairs. The burn running on the real tracks and the relief on the real field: no decorative particles, no invented weather.
